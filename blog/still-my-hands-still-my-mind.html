Still My Hands, Still My Mind

Huu Duc Nguyen 6 Nov 2026 2 min read

The tools I work with have changed many times. As a kid, I drew on every scrap of paper I could find. As a teenager, I spent nights in Photoshop and dreamed of studying computers. At university, Gemini helped me write code and polish my English and Japanese. Now Claude helps me at work every day. Things that once took me weeks now take an afternoon.

But one thing hasn't changed: the ideas and the decisions are still mine. AI can draw, code, and write, but it doesn't know what I want to make, or why. I still have to think, and I still have to put my own hands on the work to make it right.

So why would anyone hire me when they could just ask AI? Think about restaurants. Anyone can cook at home, yet people still eat out. Sometimes it's because a good chef knows which ingredients to pick, how much heat to use, and how to plate the dish so it feels right. But often the reason is simpler. Even people who cook well get tired. After a long day, they just want to sit down, order, and let someone else take care of it. They're paying for taste, but also for comfort and the time they get back.

My work is like that. My clients have the same kitchen I do, since anyone can open Claude. What many of them don't have is years of practice. Drawing, designing, and coding by hand taught me to tell good from almost-good. I know what to ask, what to throw away, and what's missing before the client notices. And even clients who use Claude well still hand the work to me. Their time is better spent on their own business, and they'd rather not worry about it. When something goes wrong, there's a real person who takes responsibility and fixes it.

The tools belong to everyone. Taste, judgment, and trust don't.
